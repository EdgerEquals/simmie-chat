A five-minute wind-down for busy brains

23 October 2026Mind & Body

Lying in bed with your brain still racing? You are definitely not the only one. Here is a short routine you can try tonight. No app needed.

Health experts generally recommend that teenagers get around 8 to 10 hours of sleep a night, but a busy head can make switching off hard. This routine takes about five minutes. Try it for a week and see how it feels.

Step 1: Dim the lights (1 minute)

Put your phone on the other side of the room, or at least face down with notifications off. Turn off bright lights and switch on a lamp. Bright screens and lights can make your brain think it is still daytime. A warm, caffeine-free drink like warm milk or a herbal tea can be a cosy signal that the day is done.

Step 2: Brain dump (2 minutes)

Grab a notebook and write down everything rattling around in your head: tomorrow's to-dos, something that annoyed you, an idea you don't want to forget. It doesn't have to be neat. Once it is on paper, your brain doesn't have to keep holding onto it.

Thoughts on paper are thoughts you don't have to hold onto.

Step 3: Slow breathing (2 minutes)

Sit or lie comfortably. Breathe in gently through your nose while you count to four, then breathe out slowly while you count to six. Breathing out for longer than you breathe in helps many people feel calmer. If your mind wanders, that's normal: just go back to counting.

Bonus: the body scan

Still awake? Starting at your toes, squeeze each part of your body for a few seconds and then let it go floppy: feet, legs, tummy, hands, shoulders, face.

When it's more than a busy brain

Everyone has the odd bad night. But if you are often lying awake worrying, or feeling down or anxious most days, you deserve support. Talk to a parent, carer, teacher or school counsellor, or reach out to one of the free services below.

Need to talk? You're not alone.

Talk to a parent, carer, teacher or school counsellor, or contact one of these free Australian services. Rebella isn't a medical service and can't give medical advice.

  • ReachOut: online support, tips and stories for young people
  • Kids Helpline: call 1800 55 1800 any time, for any reason, or chat online
  • headspace: mental health support for 12 to 25 year olds, online and in centres

If you or someone else is in danger right now, call 000.
